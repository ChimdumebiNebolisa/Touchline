# Touchline Product PRD

## 1. Product Summary

Touchline is a fictional club-football career and club management simulator. The player does not directly control footballers on the pitch. Instead, they make football decisions through a role-based career at a chosen club — as an Assistant Manager, Head Coach, or Manager.

The game is built around tactical identity, partial player knowledge, club politics, and consequences that persist across weeks and seasons. Players review the club situation, shape or recommend squad and tactical choices within their authority, prepare for matchday, and then watch outcomes through instant simulation or live tactical playback — never through arcade on-pitch controls.

Touchline is a local-first, single-player desktop experience set in a fully fictional football world. The MVP targets a coherent playable foundation: a full career loop with several management systems at foundation depth rather than fully deep simulations. The tone is mostly serious and realistic football, with believable club drama arising from football logic rather than random chaos.

## 2. One-Sentence Pitch

Touchline is a role-based fictional football career where you never touch the ball — you earn influence, make tactical decisions under pressure, and live with the consequences of a club that remembers who you are and what you promised.

## 3. Player Fantasy

The player is meant to feel like they are inside a real football club — not watching from the outside, and not playing as an individual footballer.

They hold a job with real limits. An Assistant Manager can advise but not decide. A Head Coach runs the football but not the whole club. A Manager carries the broad football project but still answers to a board, fans, and a Director of Football. Authority is not cosmetic; it changes what buttons work, what sticks, and who gets blamed.

Over time, the player influences tactics, players, staff relationships, and club pressure. They build reputation through results, professionalism, and judgment — not just wins. Decisions have downstream weight: a tactical gamble, a broken promise, a sale the fans hate, or a recommendation that proves right can all shift morale, trust, job security, and future opportunities.

The fantasy is consequence. Football is uncertain, information is incomplete, and the club reacts. The player should feel that every week matters — and that climbing from influence to control, or from a small club to a bigger one, is earned.

## 4. Target Player

Touchline is for:

- Football manager simulation fans who enjoy squad building, tactics, and club politics
- Career mode fans who want a persistent personal story across seasons and jobs
- Players who enjoy decisions, trade-offs, and consequences rather than instant gratification
- Players who care about tactical identity — how a team plays, not just whether it wins
- Sports management fans who want a focused fictional world rather than licensed real-world databases

Touchline is not aimed at players who want direct on-pitch control, quick arcade matches, or a finished Football Manager-scale simulation today.

## 5. What Touchline Is

- A fictional football management career simulator
- A role-based club career where job title changes what you can actually do
- A club identity simulator where archetype, board, fans, and Director of Football style shape pressure and expectations
- A tactical decision game where formation, style, roles, and familiarity feed into match outcomes
- A match simulation and consequence system with instant results and live tactical playback from the same underlying match
- A partial-information game where players are scouted, not fully known from day one
- A long-term career foundation with save/load, reputation, pressure, news, and job movement hooks
- A local-first, single-player desktop game in a fully fictional world

## 6. What Touchline Is Not

- Not a FIFA-style game with direct player control
- Not an arcade football game
- Not a full Football Manager clone at MVP
- Not an accounting-heavy finance simulator
- Not a complete transfer market, youth academy, media dialogue, or job-interview simulation at MVP
- Not a multiplayer or online service
- Not a licensed real-clubs or real-players product
- Not a physics-based action football game

## 7. Core Game Loop

The main loop repeats across the season:

1. **Review club situation** — Check the dashboard for objectives, pressure, morale, news, and the next fixture.
2. **Inspect squad and player information** — Browse the squad and player profiles; read known, estimated, and unknown attributes; assess form, morale, fitness, and tactical fit.
3. **Adjust or recommend tactics, training, and scouting based on role** — Set or recommend the match plan, weekly training focus, and scouting priorities within your authority.
4. **Prepare for match** — Confirm lineup and tactical setup (or file recommendations if your role limits final control).
5. **Simulate or watch the match** — Choose Instant Sim for a quick result or Live Match to watch the same simulated timeline play out visually.
6. **Review post-match consequences** — Read stats, tactical causes, player performance notes, and updates to morale, trust, reputation, and pressure.
7. **Handle pressure, morale, news, recruitment, promises, and career movement** — Respond to club events, use dashboard actions for recruitment and contracts, track job pressure, and watch career state evolve.
8. **Advance through the season** — Move through the calendar, play the next fixture, and continue building the career across weeks and seasons.

## 8. Role-Based Gameplay

Role selection is one of the most important decisions in Touchline. More authority means more accountability.

### Assistant Manager

**Fantasy:** "I am not in charge yet, but I am close enough to influence the club and earn my chance."

**What they can do:**
- Suggest lineups, tactics, and substitutions
- Recommend training focus and intensity
- Provide opposition notes and player development observations
- Report on player morale and recommend youth players
- File recruitment and contract recommendations
- Build influence through accurate advice and strong relationships

**What they cannot do:**
- Finalize lineups or tactics
- Make transfers or contract offers
- Hire staff or set board objectives
- Control press strategy or Director of Football decisions

**Why the role is interesting:** The Assistant Manager plays an influence game. Recommendations are logged but do not always become club policy. Success comes from being useful, trusted, and visible — with a hidden path toward interim or permanent promotion when senior staff leave or fail. It is the easiest route into bigger clubs, but also the role with the least direct power.

### Head Coach

**Fantasy:** "I control the football on the pitch, but I must work within the club structure."

**What they can do:**
- Control lineups, formations, tactical style, player roles, and substitutions
- Set training focus and match preparation
- Manage dressing room football matters
- Influence transfers, contracts, sales, scouting priorities, youth promotions, and staff recommendations
- Make press comments on football matters

**What they cannot fully control:**
- Final transfer fees and wage structures
- Director of Football decisions
- Board budget and ownership strategy
- Major staff hiring

**Why the role is interesting:** The Head Coach owns results on the pitch but shares the club with powerful non-football figures. Trust — from the board, players, Director of Football, and fans — can expand influence over recruitment and squad planning over time. It is the purest football role, with medium power and high blame for performance.

### Manager

**Fantasy:** "I run the football project, but the board still owns the club."

**What they can do:**
- Control lineups, tactics, and training
- Make transfers and contracts within board and wage limits
- Set scouting priorities, squad planning, and football identity
- Recommend staff and shape media direction
- Challenge Director of Football and board decisions on football matters
- Make squad role promises and sales recommendations

**What limits still exist:**
- Club ownership and board membership
- Director of Football hiring or firing
- Absolute budget ceilings
- Executive-level club decisions outside the football project

**Why the role is interesting:** The Manager is the highest playable football authority — but "highest playable" is not "total control." More power means more blame. A bad transfer, a fan backlash, or a board disagreement lands harder here. It is the fullest expression of the Touchline career fantasy, with the highest expectations and the most systems unlocked.

## 9. Club Identity System

Every club in Touchline is more than a name and a league position. Identity shapes how the club feels to manage and what kinds of decisions create praise or conflict.

**Club archetype** defines the club's broad character — for example, a Title Contender expected to win now, a Relegation Fighter where every point matters, a Youth Academy Club built around development, a Selling Club where stars may always be for sale, or a Chaotic Club where football is only half the problem. Archetype affects reputation expectations, budget feel, board patience, and baseline pressure.

**Board philosophy** defines what the board rewards and punishes. A Patient Builder tolerates slower progress; a Trigger-Happy Board reacts fast to bad runs; a Financially Strict Board cares about sustainability over sentiment. The board is a separate voice from the fans.

**Fan culture** defines what supporters emotionally react to. Results-first fans forgive boring football if you win. Attacking Football fans want excitement. Anti-Selling fans may rage at a profitable sale the board applauds. Derby Obsessed fans care disproportionately about rivalry matches.

**Director of Football style** defines a powerful internal figure who sits between the player, transfers, scouting, and squad planning. A Talent Trader wants to sell high; a Star Chaser wants big names; a Control Freak wants recruitment to run through them. Relationship with the Director affects transfer cooperation, scouting access, and internal politics.

**Objectives** give the player explicit board expectations — league position, cup progress, financial targets, or style mandates — that job security must be judged against, not results alone.

**Budget pressure** makes some clubs feel rich and impatient, others feel constrained and forced to be clever. Financial limits interact with recruitment, wage structure, and board mood.

Different clubs should feel different because the same decision — selling a star, playing youth, parking the bus, or spending big — lands differently depending on archetype, board, fans, and Director style. That tension is core to Touchline's club fantasy.

## 10. Player and Squad System

Players in Touchline are footballers with identity, not just rating numbers.

**Ability** covers core outfield and goalkeeper attributes. What matters is the distinction between true ability, what the player currently knows, and how the player performs today after form, morale, fitness, and tactical fit.

**Playing style** describes how a player naturally plays — for example, a Direct Winger, Deep Playmaker, or Target Forward.

**Tendencies** capture behavioral habits — cuts inside often, shoots early, tracks back aggressively, and similar patterns.

**Traits** mark standout characteristics — Big-Game Player, Injury Prone, Leader, Press Resistant, and others that affect match and development behavior.

**Personality** shapes reactions to benching, criticism, contracts, and manager relationships — Professional, Ambitious, Loyal, Volatile, and similar types.

**Morale, form, and fitness** are live context. A talented player in poor form, low morale, or heavy fatigue performs differently than their profile suggests.

**Contract basics** cover wage, length, role promise, and happiness — enough to create squad tension without full negotiation depth at MVP.

**Partial information** is central. Attributes may be Known (exact), Estimated (a range), or Unknown (?). Profile confidence — Low, Medium, High, or Fully Scouted — tells the player how much to trust what they see. Role, license, staff quality, and scouting effort all affect visibility.

**Scouting confidence** improves over time as assignments and staff reports reduce uncertainty.

**Tactical fit** is expressed in scouting language — how naturally a player suits the current system — not just a single compatibility number.

**Foundation depth at MVP:** Squad and player profiles should be one of the stronger systems in the first build. Injury and medical depth may be limited or abstracted. Full promise lifecycle and deep development curves are design direction for later. The UI should present rich player context even when underlying simulation depth is still foundation-level.

## 11. Tactics System

Tactics in Touchline are a stack of decisions that feed the shared match simulation.

**Formation** sets the team shape — 4-3-3, 3-5-2, and other supported setups.

**Team style** defines the broad approach — possession, counterattack, high press, direct play, and similar identities.

**Instructions** cover pressing intensity, tempo, width, risk/mentality, and other team-level sliders that shape how aggressively the team plays.

**Player roles** assign each position a tactical job — how that player is asked to behave within the system.

**Tactical familiarity** tracks how well the squad knows the current plan. A new formation or style played without training time creates gaps that show up in matches.

**Tactical fit** connects individual players to the system. A squad full of players who suit counterattacking football will struggle if the team suddenly plays heavy possession — and the post-match report should say so.

**How tactics influence match outcomes:** The match engine reads tactical setup, familiarity, player fit, morale, form, fitness, staff preparation, and opponent style together. Tactics do not guarantee wins — they shift probabilities and explain why chances were created or conceded. The player sets the plan; the simulation resolves the match; the post-match report explains the link between plan and result.

Role authority affects whether the player saves the match plan directly or files a recommendation that senior staff may ignore.

## 12. Training and Scouting

**Training focus** lets the player choose a weekly emphasis — pressing, possession, counterattack, fitness, recovery, set pieces, team cohesion, youth integration, and other areas. Intensity affects how fast familiarity and sharpness improve, but also fatigue, injury risk, and player complaints.

**Scouting focus** assigns attention to targets or regions. Over time, reports improve profile confidence and reveal estimated or known attributes.

**Progress over time** matters for both systems. Training builds tactical familiarity and player sharpness across weeks. Scouting reduces uncertainty and surfaces transfer or development opportunities.

**Staff and scouting confidence** affect report quality. Better coaching and scouting staff produce more reliable information and better training outcomes.

**Role authority:** A Head Coach or Manager sets training and scouting directly. An Assistant Manager recommends training plans and scouting priorities — the recommendation is logged, but final authority sits with senior staff. This difference should be felt in both UI feedback and whether the club actually changes course.

**Foundation depth at MVP:** Training is weekly focus and intensity, not a deep session planner. Scouting uses assignments and reports at standard depth, not a full global scouting network UI. Both systems affect state and match preparation, but dedicated full-screen depth comes later.

## 13. Matchday and Simulation

Touchline does not put the player on the pitch. Matchday is preparation, choice of viewing mode, and consequence — not direct footballer control.

**Instant Sim** runs the match and shows the result immediately — score, key events, and a path to the post-match report.

**Live Match** plays back the same simulated match as a top-down tactical view — player positions, ball movement, major events, commentary, and score/time display. It is a renderer of the simulation, not a second game.

**Shared match result and timeline:** The match is simulated first. Instant Sim and Live Match consume the same underlying timeline. Switching modes should never tell a different story — only a different pace and level of detail.

**Post-match report** explains what happened and why — stats, tactical causes, player ratings, fit notes, fatigue, morale shifts, board and fan reaction, media story, and development notes where relevant.

**Tactical explanations** connect the plan to the outcome. A high press may have created turnovers but left defensive gaps if pressing familiarity was low. The report should make that readable.

**Consequences** flow out of the match into career state — morale, trust, reputation, pressure, form, news, and objectives all update.

This is football management, not football playing. The player's skill expression is in preparation and judgment, not reflexes.

## 14. Pressure and Consequences

Touchline treats morale, trust, reputation, and pressure as separate systems. Collapsing them would flatten the club fantasy.

**Board morale** reflects how the board feels about the current situation — results, finances, style, and confidence in the manager.

**Fan morale** reflects supporter mood — influenced by results, style, player sales, youth usage, and derby outcomes.

**Squad morale** reflects dressing room mood — playing time, promises kept or broken, results, and manager relationships.

**Job pressure** tracks how close the player is to consequences — warnings, ultimatums, or sacking. It rises with poor morale, missed objectives, and club instability; it falls with trust and overachievement.

**News and events** surface the club world — internal reports, transfer rumors, board messages, and media stories with reliability labels. Events create context and sometimes force decisions.

**Player morale and form** shift after matches, training load, benching, and public comments. Individual player tension can escalate from private concern to agent pressure or transfer requests.

**Promises** — playing time, role, or squad-plan commitments — create long-term relationship debt. Breaking them damages trust; keeping them builds it.

**Career reputation** is how the wider football world sees the player — tactically, in development, in transfers, in crisis management, and in media handling. Reputation opens or closes future jobs. A license qualifies the player for bigger rooms; reputation decides whether those rooms want them.

Board approval and fan approval often disagree. A sale that satisfies a Financially Strict board may crush Anti-Selling fan morale. That conflict is intentional.

## 15. Recruitment, Contracts, Youth, Finance, and Job Market

These systems are **foundation systems** at MVP — they affect career state and dashboard context, but they are not yet full standalone gameplay loops.

### Recruitment and contracts

**MVP scope:** Dashboard recruitment and contract actions; role-appropriate recommend vs offer behavior; a current recruitment target and contract offer context; persistent transfer and contract history; news when recommendations or approaches are filed.

**Foundation depth:** No full transfer market UI with bidding wars, rival clubs, or deep negotiation trees. Transfers do not resolve on fee alone in the design intent; player-facing depth at MVP is foundation-level.

**What should deepen later:** Interactive negotiation, richer market dynamics, adaptation after signing, and clearer cause-and-effect through the full transfer lifecycle.

### Youth

**MVP scope:** Youth quality and academy quality may appear in club identity and state; youth integration appears as a training focus; youth promotion is a design hook for Head Coach and Manager influence.

**Foundation depth:** No playable youth academy loop, no youth league matches, and no full development pipeline the player manages week to week.

**What should deepen later:** A meaningful but rare youth pipeline where academy products feel special, not routine.

### Finance

**MVP scope:** Budget pressure and wage summary context visible at club level; financial pressure as a consequence category; difficulty settings that touch finance strictness.

**Foundation depth:** No full finance ledger, no wage negotiation UI, and no deep budget simulation the player manages line by line.

**What should deepen later:** Readable financial trade-offs — sustainability vs ambition — without becoming an accounting simulator.

### Job market

**MVP scope:** Dashboard job-market section; career state for reputation, license, and job movement hooks; job pressure and sacking foundations.

**Foundation depth:** No full job interview flow, no global manager carousel the player navigates screen by screen, and no deep hiring negotiation.

**What should deepen later:** Career progression that feels earned — interviews, club interest, interim opportunities, and the Assistant Manager caretaker path.

## 16. MVP Scope and Foundation Systems

The MVP delivers a **coherent single-player career slice**, not a finished commercial football management product. The first playable build should prove the core fantasy end to end; many management systems ship at foundation depth with clear room to deepen later.

**MVP must include:**
- New career and continue career from the main menu
- Career setup with role, manager background, license, difficulty options, and world seed
- Club selection with identity and pressure preview
- Club dashboard with pressure, morale, objectives, news, and next-action context
- Squad and player profiles with partial information (Known / Estimated / Unknown, profile confidence)
- Tactics with role-appropriate save or recommend flows
- Training and scouting dashboard actions
- Recruitment, contracts, and job-market dashboard sections
- Fixtures, standings, and matchday flow
- Instant Sim and Live Match tactical playback from the same underlying match simulation
- Post-match report with stats, causes, and consequences
- Save/load and persistent career continuity
- Season progression in a seeded fictional league
- Role-specific authority differences across Assistant Manager, Head Coach, and Manager

**Foundation-depth at MVP (present but simplified):**
- UI should be readable at 1280×720 with practical football-management layout — not decorative dashboard slop
- Initial demo world may be a small seeded league rather than a full multi-division pyramid
- Transfers, contracts, job market, training, scouting, finance, youth, media dialogue, and job interviews are foundation-depth rather than complete simulations
- Promotions, relegation, cups, and multi-competition calendars are not required for the first MVP slice

The honest MVP promise: a coherent single-player career where role, tactics, partial information, match consequences, and save/load work end to end — with many management systems present as hooks for future depth.

## 17. MVP Definition

The MVP is:

**A playable single-user fictional football career simulator where the user can start a career, choose a role and club, inspect squad and tactics, simulate matches, see consequences, and save/load a persistent career.**

If a player can complete that loop without confusion and feel that their role mattered, the MVP is doing its job.

## 18. Out of Scope for MVP

The following are explicitly out of scope for the current MVP:

- Full transfer market depth
- Full youth academy depth
- Full finance ledger
- Full media dialogue trees
- Full job interviews
- Multiplayer
- Licensed real clubs and players
- Direct player control on the pitch
- Online services, cloud saves, or accounts

## 19. Success Criteria

Success is measured from the player's perspective:

- The player understands their role and its limits within about two minutes of starting a career.
- The dashboard makes the club situation clear — what matters this week, what the board expects, and where pressure is coming from.
- The player can complete a full match cycle — prepare, play, read post-match — without confusion about what happened or why.
- The post-match report explains why the result happened in terms the player can connect to their tactical choices.
- Role authority feels meaningfully different across Assistant Manager, Head Coach, and Manager — not just label text.
- Save/load works reliably; continuing a career feels safe and state-complete.
- After a session, the player wants to continue one more week.

## 20. Next Product Priorities

Product priorities for what Touchline should become next — not engineering tasks:

1. **Make UI fully readable and visually designed at 1280×720** — Final visual acceptance of the rebuilt shell, cards, and screen hierarchy.
2. **Make the 30-minute personal play loop satisfying** — A new player should reach matchday, see consequences, and feel hooked within half an hour.
3. **Improve post-match explanation and consequence clarity** — The link between tactics, events, and downstream morale/pressure should be obvious and trustworthy.
4. **Deepen training and scouting decisions** — Weekly choices should feel like they matter more than a single dropdown.
5. **Make recruitment and contracts feel more interactive** — Move from foundation dashboard actions toward real trade-offs and outcomes.
6. **Improve career progression and job-market feel** — Reputation, license, and job pressure should connect to tangible next-club opportunities.
7. **Record a clean portfolio demo path** — A repeatable new-career-to-post-match flow suitable for showing the product to others.

## 21. Demo Pitch

Here is a two-minute walkthrough of Touchline:

"You start Touchline and begin a new career. You pick your name, your role — Assistant Manager, Head Coach, or Manager — your background, and your coaching license. Each role is a different job, not a difficulty setting.

You choose a fictional club and immediately see what kind of club it is — its archetype, what the board cares about, what the fans expect, and who your Director of Football is. Every club has a different personality.

You land on the dashboard. This is home base — objectives, pressure, morale from the board, fans, and squad, the next fixture, and the latest news. From here you browse your squad. Players are not fully known. Some attributes are exact, some are ranges, some are question marks. You are a manager working with incomplete information, not a spreadsheet.

You go to tactics. Depending on your role, you either set the match plan or recommend one. You choose formation, style, and instructions — this is what your team takes into the match engine.

It's matchday. You can Instant Sim for a quick result or switch to Live Match and watch the same simulated game play out from above — same timeline, same result, just a different way of experiencing it. You never control a player directly.

After the whistle, the post-match report tells you what happened and why — stats, tactical causes, player performance, and how the board, fans, and squad reacted. Morale, trust, reputation, and job pressure all shift.

You save your career and come back tomorrow. That is Touchline — a role-based football career where decisions, incomplete information, and club identity all matter."

## 22. Portfolio Summary

**What the project is:** Touchline is a fictional club-football career and management simulator — a coherent playable slice of a larger design with honest foundation depth.

**Core systems:** Role-based authority (Assistant Manager, Head Coach, Manager); club identity (archetype, board, fans, Director of Football); partial player information; tactics and tactical familiarity; shared match simulation with Instant Sim and Live Match playback; post-match consequences; morale, trust, reputation, and pressure as separate systems; save/load career persistence.

**Platform:** Local-first, single-player desktop game. No backend, accounts, or online services required.

**Why it matters:** Touchline combines spec-driven product design with an end-to-end career loop. Role authority should be enforced in gameplay, not just UI labels. One match simulation should serve both instant and live viewing. Partial information, club identity, and consequence systems are designed as interconnected product logic — not isolated screens.

## 23. Research and Inspiration References

Future Touchline work should learn from these repos and resources without copying code blindly. They are research inputs for product quality, architecture discipline, UI taste, and management-simulation feel — not shortcuts to paste into the codebase.

### 1. Uncodixfy

- **URL:** https://github.com/cyxzdev/Uncodixfy
- **Use:** anti-AI-slop UI guardrails
- **Lesson:** avoid generic GPT UI patterns, oversized cards, gradient-heavy dashboards, decorative labels, glass panels, and fake-polished layouts.
- **Apply to Touchline:** future UI must be practical, readable, football-management focused, and not decorative AI dashboard slop.

### 2. Godot Demo Projects

- **URL:** https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects
- **Use:** Godot project and UI implementation reference
- **Lesson:** use real Godot scene/container patterns instead of dumping UI text into panels.
- **Apply to Touchline:** future implementation should study Godot UI examples before building screens.

### 3. Game Programming Patterns

- **URL:** https://github.com/munificent/game-programming-patterns
- **Use:** game architecture and system design reference
- **Lesson:** game loops, state, event queues, update methods, observers, and component-style thinking help keep game logic clear.
- **Apply to Touchline:** future implementation should separate product systems from UI presentation.

### 4. OpenRCT2

- **URL:** https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2
- **Use:** management simulation inspiration
- **Lesson:** management games need clear objectives, readable status, scenario/sandbox framing, reputation/guest/player feedback, and understandable consequences.
- **Apply to Touchline:** club pressure, morale, board/fan reactions, and match consequences should be readable like a serious management sim.

### 5. OpenTTD

- **URL:** https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD
- **Use:** simulation-game documentation and long-term project reference
- **Lesson:** explain gameplay clearly, document manuals/help, protect save compatibility, and keep simulation systems understandable.
- **Apply to Touchline:** future builds need strong player-facing help and persistent-career stability.

### 6. FreeOrion

- **URL:** https://github.com/freeorion/freeorion
- **Use:** grand-campaign strategy simulation inspiration
- **Lesson:** fictional worldbuilding, campaign progression, scripting/content separation, and strategy depth can create a living long-term game world.
- **Apply to Touchline:** clubs, leagues, players, staff, and career reputation should feel like a living fictional football world.

### 7. LinCity-NG

- **URL:** https://github.com/lincity-ng/lincity-ng
- **Use:** management simulation framing
- **Lesson:** a sim can be complex while still having simple player goals and clear success/failure framing.
- **Apply to Touchline:** the player should always understand what they are trying to achieve this week, this season, and in their career.

### 8. Battle for Wesnoth

- **URL:** https://github.com/wesnoth/wesnoth
- **Use:** player-facing game documentation and long-running open-source game structure
